Guide

How the game works

I. The Beach (12/13 and 16/17 June 1942)

Amagansett. You run the Coast Guard station on Long Island for one night. You set the watch, decide whom to call when Seaman Cullen comes back with the strangers' money, where to send your men, what to do with the finds at first light and what the men may say. The record's times – 00:10, 01:05, 01:55, after 02:30 – come into the chronicle as you go. Every patrol is unarmed; the investigation of enemy agents belongs to the FBI.

Ponte Vedra. You are at the naval section base at Mayport, at the mouth of the St. Johns River, from 20:00 to 06:00. Each hour, give the two patrol boats YP-31 and YP-32 an area. Before the night you may ask the Army to run part of its motor patrol south of Ponte Vedra, and post two volunteers on the beach. A boat may stay out eight hours. Somebody may see something – rarely. Historically nobody did.

II. The Call (14–27 June 1942)

Each turn is a day at the FBI. Choose two things to do: canvass Long Island, work the evidence, watch known contacts, share with the Navy and Coast Guard, keep the press out – and, if the beach in Florida gave you a lead, search Jacksonville. The decisions come as they came: the call at 19:51 on Sunday 14 June, the man in Washington, the three in New York, the handkerchief in secret ink, the boxes in Florida, telling the Navy, Chicago. What happened in 1942 runs in the chronicle as 'The record'; what happens in your game is marked 'Game'.

III. The Credit (27 June – 8 August 1942)

The press arrives as it came – the Sunday Star of 28 June, the AP reports of 29 June, the President's proclamation, the Coast Guard's own story in July. You decide what the Director says about how the eight were found, how much credit the Coast Guard gets, what to do with the rumour of 'a lone unarmed Coast Guardsman', how the agents answer before the commission, and whether to ask mercy for the two who helped.

The fixed record

The military commission, its verdicts, and the deaths of six men on 8 August 1942 are the record, never a move. The game shows no photograph of the dead.
