## Seitentext

**Without Any Action**
*Operation Pastorius, Long Island and Florida, June 1942*

"This memo is being prepared only for the purpose of recording the call." – FBI New York, 14 June 1942.

In June 1942 two German U-boats put eight saboteurs ashore with explosives and American money. Four landed at Amagansett on Long Island, where a Coast Guard patrolman met them in the fog. Four landed at Ponte Vedra Beach in Florida, where nobody saw them at all. Two weeks later all eight were in custody, and the FBI told the country how it had caught them. The record tells it differently. One of the eight had telephoned the FBI himself, and the call had been filed without any action.

**Three scenarios on the American side.**
- **I. The Beach.**
  - **Amagansett, 12/13 June:** you run a Coast Guard station for one night. You set the watch, decide whom to call when the patrolman comes back with the strangers' money, and choose what to do with the finds.
  - **Ponte Vedra, 16/17 June:** you are at the naval section base at Mayport. Each hour you send two patrol boats where you think best, and you may ask the Army for help. Somebody may see something, but rarely; historically nobody did.
- **II. The Call (14–27 June):** the FBI, day by day.
  - the call at 19:51 on a Sunday;
  - the man who walks in in Washington;
  - the three in New York;
  - the handkerchief in secret ink;
  - the boxes in the Florida sand;
  - Chicago.
- **III. The Credit (27 June – 8 August):** what the Director says about how they were found, how much credit the Coast Guard gets, and whether to ask mercy for the two who helped.

**The reckoning** sets four versions side by side: what the FBI said, what the record says, what the Coast Guard said, and what the beach at Ponte Vedra saw.

- Nobody fights in this game. The military commission, its verdicts and the deaths of 8 August 1942 are fixed record, never a move. No photograph of the dead is shown.
- Every card names its source. Most come from the transcript of the military commission, the FBI's New York file and the naval war diaries, read at the page images.
- 12 plates, all US government works in the public domain, including the FBI memo of 14 June and the Mayport war diary for the night of the landing.
- Free to play.
